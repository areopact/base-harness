# Education outline mode

Use this reference only for a teaching deck, workshop, lecture, or training session.

## Brief and research

Resolve the audience's baseline, what they should understand or be able to do afterward, duration, venue, and whether a live demonstration is appropriate. Use a felt problem or recognizable situation before definitions. Reverify version-sensitive facts close to delivery and mark unverifiable claims as uncitable.

## Teaching arc

- Hook with the learner's problem, reveal the thesis, then establish shared vocabulary.
- Give the thesis-bearing act the most time. Do not make every act symmetrical by habit.
- Carry one or two recurring examples through the deck so setup pays off later.
- State literally true qualifiers on slides. Put mechanism, nuance, and likely objections in speaker cues.
- End with a concrete synthesis or meta-proof, then the learner's next action.

## Slide-map additions

For each slide include the learner change, one teaching message, evidence, a visual or example, timing, and speaker cues. Use `SAY:` for delivery beats and `BACKGROUND:` for mechanism, caveats, and pocket answers.

## Demonstrations

Place each demo where it proves a teaching point, not where it adds spectacle. Record:

- the claim or skill the demo proves
- live steps and narration anchors
- dependencies and ordered pre-bake steps
- one local pre-baked fallback per live step
- an honest failure exhibit when failure detection is part of the lesson

The outline must survive no internet. Demo placeholders are labeled; generated media and statistics are never fabricated.
