# Introduction outline mode

Use this reference for a concise introduction to a person, team, company, project, product, or idea when the goal is orientation and a next action rather than teaching or fundraising.

## Required frame

The outline answers four questions in order:

1. What is the subject?
2. Why does it matter to this audience now?
3. What value, evidence, or distinctive mechanism should they remember?
4. What should they do next?

Open with the audience's context, not an internal origin story. Explain only the background needed to understand the value. Use real examples and evidence where available; label aspiration as aspiration.

## Slide map

A short introduction usually needs a cover, audience-relevant context, the subject and mechanism, proof or example, and a closing next action. Change the count when the content requires it. Each slide still carries one message, cited claims or explicit gaps, a visual suggestion, timing, and speaker cues.

The close names one primary next action and the responsible person or team when known. Do not end with a menu of unrelated asks.
