# Output Discipline

Two output-shaping rules that refine, without replacing, the universal contract in [output-quality](output-quality.md): truth over punch in educational content, and no em-dashes anywhere.

## Why

Absolutes read well and are usually falsifiable. In teaching material the audience contains the exceptions, and one catchable overstatement undermines the teacher's credibility, most of all in content that itself teaches verification and skepticism. The em-dash has become the single most recognizable typographic tell of machine-generated prose; restructuring around it usually yields a tighter sentence anyway.

## Application

### Truth over punch

In educational content (slides, training decks, explainers), prefer literally true statements over punchier absolutes. Downgrade "never / always / nobody" to the accurate quantifier ("rarely / almost always / most") unless the absolute is literally true. Scan headlines and claims in educational artifacts for absolutes and downgrade them; put nuance and exceptions in speaker notes or body text. Marketing copy may keep the punch; education gets the truth.

### No em-dashes

Refrain from em-dashes and en-dashes in generated prose. Where a dash would carry a parenthetical, use commas or parentheses; where it would introduce an elaboration, use a colon; where it marks an abrupt turn, split into two sentences. If a dash is genuinely needed, use a spaced hyphen ( - ). This governs output going forward, not a retroactive scrub of existing files. A writing sample supplied by the author outranks this rule; see Precedence in [output-quality](output-quality.md).
